Cogwheel Marketing’s hotel SEO study: 144 to 178 top-ten keywords

Cogwheel Marketing reports wider keyword coverage and more organic visitors for a hotel. This external case distinguishes the campaign’s ranking comparison from its year-over-year traffic comparison.

By DotMiracle · 2 min read
DOTMIRACLE / FIELD SERIES

A hotel discovery and optimisation programme

Cogwheel Marketing describes an eight-month programme combining a technical baseline, keyword research, on-page changes, local profile work, and link development. DotMiracle did not deliver this engagement. We use it to illustrate how a hotel can report discovery without mistaking traffic for bookings.

Original hotel case study — Cogwheel Marketing

Two comparisons, three measured outcomes

Traffic uses matched March–September periods in different years. Keyword coverage uses the beginning and end of the March–October programme. These time windows are not interchangeable.

MeasureBeforeAfterDifference
Organic visitors, March–September26,972 in 202337,673 in 202410,701 more; approximately 40%
Keywords in the top ten, campaign comparison14417834 additional top-ten terms
Total ranking keywords, March to October1,8002,8211,021 additional ranking terms

What the results do not establish

A count of top-ten keywords is not an average position and does not mean every term improved. The report does not provide matching before-and-after totals for booking enquiries or confirmed stays. We therefore make no numerical claim about lead growth, direct bookings, or revenue for this hotel.

A useful comparison for Kerala stays

Alongside matched-period organic traffic, record room availability, major promotional activity, enquiries, confirmed reservations, and cancellations. A resort can then determine whether better discovery brought guests it could actually accommodate.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

The practical checklist

  • Compare equivalent months when reporting seasonal traffic.
  • Name the ranking metric: keyword count, position, or visibility.
  • Measure enquiries and completed stays rather than inferring them from visits.
