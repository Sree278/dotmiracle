# Customer journey: hypotheses to validate

These scenarios are assumptions, not findings. Priorities reflect the likely importance of the decision to a visitor, not measured traffic or conversion data.

| Priority | Visitor and situation | Positive motivation | Possible concern | Content response | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | SME owner seeking SEO help | More relevant discovery and enquiries | Previous SEO spend did not produce clear results; fear of guaranteed-ranking claims | SEO questions explain measurement and realistic expectations; link to the qualified Mindtech case study | Ask prior clients how they assessed SEO and what evidence would build trust |
| High | Founder planning SaaS | Bring an idea to market | Building the wrong thing, uncertain scope, cost or timeline | Explain defining a first release and agreeing scope; answer common questions | Ask founders what they needed to decide before seeking development |
| High | Marketing lead reviewing a website | A site that better represents the business | Disruption, content migration, ownership and support | Explain that launch, handover and ownership should be agreed in the project scope | Interview website clients about their concerns before and after launch |
| High | Interested visitor considering contact | Wants help with a clear challenge | Unclear next steps or no functioning contact route | Explain the intended process; mark the form unavailable until connected | Connect and test a contact channel first; then ask prospects what made them contact or hesitate |
| Medium | Visitor unsure which service fits | Wants direction without committing | Unfamiliar terminology or too many choices | Goal-based paths and a low-pressure link to the approach page | Observe whether people can find a relevant next step without assistance |
| Medium | Prospect outside Chennai | Wants a suitable partner | Unsure whether remote collaboration is possible | Describe service coverage and remote collaboration without claiming unverified offices | Ask prospects where they are and whether location influenced their decision |

## Research and measurement

1. Speak with a small mix of past clients and prospects. Ask what prompted their search, what alternatives they considered, what concerned them, and what information helped them decide. Record their words with permission; do not treat a small sample as representative.
2. After a working, tested contact route exists, establish a baseline for relevant page views, case-study engagement and completed enquiries. Decide on a privacy-conscious analytics setup and applicable consent requirements before collecting data. Do not record form contents or personal data in analytics events.
3. Change one meaningful element at a time where practical. Compare like-for-like periods and note traffic source, sample size and context; do not claim an improvement from a handful of enquiries.
4. Review findings and update or discard these hypotheses. Search-friendly individual URLs remain a separate technical task; hash routes are not separate indexable pages.

No interviews, tracking or measurement have been conducted as part of this change. The contact form is currently a non-sending preview.
